\section{Implementation}
Both tasks use PyTorch Monte Carlo policy gradients and complete episodes. CartPole uses \texttt{CartPole-v1}; Pong uses \texttt{ALE/Pong-v5}, the namespaced Pong-v5 environment. Actions are sampled during training and evaluation.

For decision $t$, $s_t$ is the observed input, $a_t$ the action, $r_t$ its subsequent reward, and $T$ the episode length. The policy $\pi_\theta(a\mid s)$ is a neural network with parameters $\theta$. With discount $\gamma$, the objective and reward-to-go are
\[
J(\theta)=\mathbb E_{\pi_\theta}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^t r_t\right],
\qquad G_t=\sum_{k=t}^{T-1}\gamma^{k-t}r_k.
\]
For a batch of $B$ episodes, Adam minimizes
\[
\mathcal L=-\frac{c}{B}\sum_{i=1}^{B}\sum_{t=0}^{T_i-1}
\gamma^t\log\pi_\theta(a_t^{(i)}\mid s_t^{(i)})(G_t^{(i)}-b_t^{(i)}).
\]
Here $i$ indexes episodes, $b_t$ is the baseline (zero in Part 1), and $c$ is a fixed scale. Returns and baselines are detached from actor gradients; no return centering or standardization is used. Plots show \emph{undiscounted} episode rewards, as requested.

Pong uses only actions 2 and 3 (RIGHT and LEFT), with no startup FIRE/NOOP. Frames are cropped and downsampled to $80\times80$, backgrounds 144/109 removed, and foreground binarized using the assignment's preprocessing. Consecutive binary frames are subtracted to supply motion; the reset input is zero. Default frame skip 4 and sticky-action probability 0.25 are retained. Full games are episodes: returns never reset at points. Gradients accumulate over all eight games before one update.

Pong's alternative rate 0.001 gives a smaller update: on identical game data, mean policy KL divergence after one update was $1.996\times10^{-6}$, versus $3.423\times10^{-4}$ at 0.01. This motivates a conservative rate but does not establish better long-run stability. Clipping affected 61.68\% of basic Pong updates and 18.64\% with a baseline; CartPole never reached its cap.

\textbf{Evaluation.} Each main result uses the final fixed-budget checkpoint and 500 fresh episodes, without learning. Test reset seeds are separate from training and shared across methods. CartPole seed 1668 was selected before training for the main plots; all three seeds are reported below. SD denotes population SD across the 500 episode rewards.

\section{Part 2(b): Pong with a baseline}
The baseline is a lagged scalar exponential moving average. Update $k$ uses $b_k$, starting at $b_0=0$; afterward,
\[
\bar G_k=\frac{\sum_{i,t}\gamma^tG_t^{(i)}}{\sum_{i,t}\gamma^t},
\qquad b_{k+1}=0.95b_k+0.05\bar G_k.
\]
Thus the baseline uses only previous batches when forming an actor gradient. An action-independent baseline preserves the expected unclipped gradient because
$\mathbb E_a[b\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)]=b\nabla_\theta\sum_a\pi_\theta(a\mid s)=0$.
Here $\nabla_\theta$ denotes differentiation with respect to policy parameters. Clipping can alter this expectation.

The final score was \textbf{\PongBaselineMean{} $\pm$ \PongBaselineSD}, only 0.164 above Part 1. All 500 games were losses (range $-21$ to $-5$), with no truncations. One training seed cannot establish a general benefit.

\subsection*{Were CartPole's dips just episode noise?}
Both original seed-1668 runs were replayed exactly: all 8,000 rewards and final parameter hashes matched. Frozen checkpoints before and during selected dips were then evaluated on 500 fresh paired reset/action seeds. Basic-policy means fell from 453.108 (episode 5,120) to 83.114 (6,400): paired change $-369.994$, bootstrap 95\% interval $[-377.430,-362.038]$. Baseline-policy means fell from 440.466 (3,072) to 215.686 (6,144): change $-224.780$, interval $[-231.886,-217.436]$.

Holding the earlier basic policy fixed for 2,000 fresh episodes gave 100-episode averages of 430.11--472.29; continued training reached 74.64. The baseline's frozen range was 413.00--453.82, versus a continued-training minimum of 211.37. Policy quality changed beyond ordinary fixed-policy episode noise. Noisy updates may cause such changes, but this experiment does not identify the optimization mechanism. Checkpoints were selected after inspecting training curves; intervals are conditional on these policies, not uncertainty across training seeds.

\subsection*{What the discounted objective rewards}
CartPole's discounted return is $(1-0.95^T)/0.05$, approaching 20 even for episodes shorter than 500. The primary baseline model averaged 19.999743 despite its lower undiscounted score. In Pong, discounted evaluation return improved from $-1.309180$ at initialization to 0.313913 (basic) and 0.293346 (baseline), while full-game scores remained poor. At $\gamma=0.99$, rewards 1,000 decisions away receive weight $4.32\times10^{-5}$. This suggests a credit-assignment limitation, but is not a causal test.

\section{Edge cases and quantitative validation}
\subsection*{CartPole: both methods}
\textbf{Returns and gradient.} Rewards $(1,1,1)$ at $\gamma=0.95$ must give returns $(2.8525,1.95,1)$; the code matches. Setting $\gamma=0$ gives immediate rewards. In an enumerable two-step task with binary-action reward and action probability $p=\operatorname{sigmoid}(\theta)$, the analytic gradient is $(1+\gamma)p(1-p)$. Enumeration gave 0.471188756 versus analytic 0.471188748, with and without a constant baseline. A rewarded-action update increased its probability from 0.500000 to 0.502500, checking the loss sign.

\textbf{End conditions.} Forced cart-position and pole-angle violations terminated the episode and retained the terminal $+1$ reward. A one-step time limit truncated without physical failure. Either flag ends return accumulation, with zero continuation. Fixed reset seeds and ten fixed actions reproduced identical states. The saved ten-decision trace records states, action probabilities, actions and rewards for inspection.

\textbf{Baseline mechanism.} At a frozen initial actor, a baseline fitted on 512 separate episodes was evaluated on 512 paired held-out trajectories. Summed parameter-gradient variances fell from 5.338262 to 0.842421 (84.22\%). The empirical mean-gradient difference was 0.0474, versus paired standard-error magnitude 0.1176. This supports variance reduction at that actor; it does not validate sustained reduction by the critic's five-step online fitting schedule. The controlled replay experiment above separately tests whether training dips reflect real policy changes.

\subsection*{Pong: both methods}
\textbf{Episode boundaries and actions.} Rewards $(0,1,0,-1)$ at $\gamma=0.99$ must yield
\[ (G_0,G_1,G_2,G_3)=(0.019701,0.0199,-0.99,-1). \]
The implementation matched within $10^{-6}$. This checks that returns cross scoring boundaries. Zero discount recovered immediate rewards; the two-step analytic-gradient test agreed within $10^{-7}$. Zero returns produced zero basic-policy gradient. Complete games reached scoring events and termination using only actions 2/3; workers reject other actions.

\textbf{Images and replay.} Known synthetic pixels verified crop, downsampling, background removal and foreground preservation without modifying the source image. Real inputs were zero at reset and otherwise in $\{-1,0,1\}$. Resetting frame history prevents leakage across games. Identical emulator seeds and fixed actions reproduced frame/reward hashes with sticky actions retained. Saved traces include the first ten decisions, a scoring event and the ending, with corresponding image differences.

\textbf{Baseline mechanism.} A separately fitted initial constant reduced summed gradient variance from 369.320 to 20.763 on 16 held-out games (94.38\%). This candidate is distinct from the training EMA, which starts at zero. At 4,096, 8,192, 12,288 and 16,384 games, the actual EMA's paired variance ratios (with/without baseline, at the same frozen actor) were 0.873, 1.017, 0.924 and 1.011. It did not consistently reduce variance. These small 16-game probes have no variance-ratio confidence intervals and measure unclipped gradients.

\textbf{Recovery and resources.} Restoring actor and Adam state and repeating the next fixed-data update reproduced parameter hashes exactly. Preflight passed all 15 checks and verified one visible GPU. Both methods used that GPU sequentially with eight CPU emulator workers; training and evaluation completed in 2.64 hours. These checks support implementation correctness, while the game scores establish that learning remained inadequate.

\section{Run instructions}
Paths below are relative to the repository's \texttt{assignment1/} directory. Use a fresh copy for training to preserve saved results. Python 3.12, PyTorch 2.14.0, NumPy 2.5.3 and Gymnasium 1.2.2 were used; Pong also used ALE 0.11.2.

\textbf{CartPole:} install requirements, train all six models, evaluate each for 500 episodes, then run the numerical and environment tests:
\begin{verbatim}
cd mlds_assignment1_cartpole
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python cartpole.py suite --episodes 8000 --lr 0.01
python validate.py
jupyter lab cartpole_plots.ipynb
\end{verbatim}
Validation evidence is in \texttt{validation/validation.json}; the notebook plots saved results without retraining. The controlled dip experiment is reproduced with
\texttt{python instability\_experiment.py}, followed by
\texttt{python plot\_instability\_experiment.py}; see its saved \texttt{plan.json} and README for seeds and bootstrap details. The separate \texttt{cartpole\_lr005} and \texttt{cartpole\_lr002} folders each contain \texttt{run.sh}, which runs the six lower-rate experiments, validation and plotting.

\textbf{Pong:} in a fresh directory containing its source, config and requirements, activate a Python environment and install requirements. Select one GPU; set its UUID in \texttt{config.json} and \texttt{supervise.sh}. Then:
\begin{verbatim}
export CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=<single-GPU-UUID>
export CUBLAS_WORKSPACE_CONFIG=:4096:8
export OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1
python run_pong.py preflight
nohup setsid bash supervise.sh > nohup.log 2>&1 < /dev/null &
\end{verbatim}
Preflight executes the Pong checks above. Existing checkpoints resume rather than restart. See \texttt{preflight/results.json}, \texttt{runs/*/training.csv},
\texttt{runs/*/evaluation/test\_final.json}, and \texttt{diagnostics/} for quantitative evidence. The source README documents recovery and traces.

\textbf{Report:} run \texttt{python build\_assets.py}, then compile
\texttt{POLICY\_GRADIENT\_REPORT.tex} with Tectonic or pdfLaTeX. Run
\texttt{python combine\_submission.py} to join the shuttle and policy-gradient PDFs.

\section{Experiments with three additional months}
\textbf{CartPole:} use more independent training seeds and separate tuning/test resets. Compare learning rates, batch sizes and baseline fitting schedules under matched budgets; measure critic error and paired gradient variance throughout training. Stress-test initial angles, velocities and altered physical parameters to assess generalization beyond standard resets.

\textbf{Pong:} keep v5, actions 2/3 and $\gamma=0.99$ while comparing frame differences against frame stacks and a small convolutional policy. Measure gradient contributions by episode time to test delayed-credit limitations. Tune learning rate, clipping and batch size on validation seeds, then repeat training across seeds with untouched 500-game tests.

\textbf{Baselines:} compare scalar, state-dependent and time-dependent predictions at identical frozen actors, fitting on independent data. Use hundreds of paired trajectories and bootstrap variance-ratio intervals. Compare clipped and unclipped estimators to determine whether variance reduction translates into better control.
